    How Magic Works

    Spells follow the 2024 Player's Handbook. Every spell's full text is on its level's page (Spells by Class lists who can learn what). This page covers how casting works in this game, and where it plays differently from the book (House Rules has the full list).

    Spell slots, preparing and resting

    • Spell slots come back on a Long Rest (a Warlock's Pact Magic slots on a Short Rest too). Rest with R.
    • Prepared spells can be changed any time outside a fight, with no rest needed (a house rule): on the character sheet's Spells tab (C), on the rest screen, or from the camp panel (Resting and Saving). Clerics, Druids, Wizards, Paladins and Rangers can swap as many as they like, and a Wizard or High Elf their cantrip too. The same goes for a Circle of the Land Druid's land and a Scion of the Three's allegiance. Always-prepared spells stay, and slots already spent don't come back. Bards, Sorcerers and Warlocks still change spells when they level up.
    • Wizards prepare from their spellbook: six level 1 spells to start and two more each level. A spellbook or scroll you find can be copied into it from the inventory: 2 hours and 50 gp per spell level, for a level you can prepare.
    • Spells that last 8 hours (Mage Armor, Aid, Darkvision, Death Ward, Alarm, Leomund's Tiny Hut and the rest) last until the next Long Rest instead of eight hours on the clock.
    • Mage Armor can be set to cast itself as each Long Rest ends, so nobody has to remember it in the morning.

    Casting in a fight

    • Spells sit on the hotbar: number keys 1 to 0, Z and X to change tab. Pick a spell, then click a target or a square; T cycles targets and Enter confirms.
    • To cast with a higher slot (upcasting), raise or lower the slot level with [ and ] before you cast.
    • Aiming an area spell draws its shape on the ground and lists every creature it would catch (below).
    • Hex and Hunter's Mark move when their target drops: a Move action appears on the hotbar once the marked creature is down, and Hex's ability curse moves with it. Hunter's Mark needs a new quarry you can see.
    • Teleporting (Misty Step and the like) frees you from webs, nets, entangling plants and grapples. Dimension Door and Thunder Step can take one willing ally along: after you pick the square, the allies within 5 ft light up; click one (or press A) to bring them, or press Enter (Y) to go alone. Thunder Step's companion has to be your size or smaller, and the boom it leaves behind is drawn and listed like any other area. Far Step keeps a teleport on the hotbar: a Bonus Action each turn while you concentrate.
    • Sculpt Spells: an Evoker picks which creatures a sculpted spell spares, and can spare themselves too (a house rule).
    • Concentration works as in the book: one spell at a time, and taking damage calls for a Constitution save (DC 10 or half the damage, whichever is higher, up to 30).
    • Drop Concentration ends a spell at will. On the concentrating creature's own turn it sits on the hotbar's Common tab, named for the spell. It asks first, then ends the spell and everything it keeps going (a banished creature comes back, a cloud clears, Haste's lethargy follows). It costs no action, Bonus Action, Reaction or movement.
    • Reactions and choices made mid-roll (Shield, Counterspell, Heroic Inspiration, a Bardic Inspiration die and the like) follow each hero's rule on the class tab of the character sheet: Ask, Automatic or Never (below).
    • Preserve Life (a Life Domain Cleric's Channel Divinity) opens a prompt with a slider for each Bloodied creature within 30 ft. Share out the pool of 5 × your Cleric level Hit Points, up to half of each one's maximum; the count of what's left falls as you give. Most hurt first puts back the default split, Heal confirms and Skip spends nothing. Undead and Constructs can't be healed by it, and what you don't share is lost.
    • Divine Smite uses the older rule the game's owner prefers: it isn't a spell and costs no Bonus Action, so a Paladin can smite every melee hit while slots last. By default the game asks after each hit. Arm it on the Spells tab to smite every hit that turn, or set it to Automatic or Off on the class tab.

    Aiming areas and lines

    Aiming an area spell draws its edge on the ground as a pale gold line, and paints no squares: a circle for a radius (Fireball), a triangle for a cone (Burning Hands) and a square for a cube (Thunderwave, Hypnotic Pattern). The same line flashes when the spell goes off. A panel beside it lists every creature the spell would catch, with each one's chance to save. Walls still outline their squares.

    Emanations and auras are round too. Spirit Guardians, a Paladin's Aura of Protection and a monster's aura reach everything within their size measured straight from their source, so the circle on the floor shows exactly who's inside. A 5-ft aura covers the same squares as before; from 10 ft up, the far diagonal corners fall outside. In a fight a Paladin's aura shows as a patch on the floor around them, with no glow round the Paladin.

    Line spells (Lightning Bolt, Gust of Wind, Sunbeam, a Dragonborn's breath line) aim at any angle. Point the mouse, or the left stick on a controller, and the line runs straight out from the middle of the caster's space. A square is caught when at least half of it lies inside the line, the DMG's grid rule. Within 2° of a row, column or diagonal the line snaps to lie exactly along it. Monsters' lines work the same way (House Rules).

    Cones and Thunderwave's cube aim at any angle too, with the mouse or the left stick in one-degree steps, and snap the same way near a row, column or diagonal. A cone starts where the aim leaves the caster's space and takes every square at least a quarter inside it; the cube takes the squares whose middles it covers. Foes' cones point straight at the creature they're aimed at (House Rules).

    Reactions

    A hero set to Ask who has more than one Reaction that fits the moment gets one prompt listing them all. Pick one and choose Use Reaction, or Skip to keep the Reaction for later. During an attack the prompt also lists what could come later in it, such as Sentinel, if the attack hits: picking that keeps the Reaction for the Opportunity Attack after a hit, and if the attack misses the hero still has it. So a Paladin with the Protection fighting style and the Sentinel feat (Feats) is asked once, not once for each. On a controller, A on an option picks it. Hellish Rebuke and Backlash set to Automatic still ask which way to cast them when there's more than one: each slot level you have, or a free casting. Backlash's choices show the dice it cuts from the damage and lashes back with.

    • Absorb Elements (Druids, Rangers, Sorcerers and Wizards) answers acid, cold, fire, lightning or thunder damage: you resist that type for the blow and until your next turn starts, and your first melee hit on that turn deals an extra 1d6 of it (+1d6 for each slot level above 1). The prompt lists each slot level and any free casting with the dice it stores, and asks even on Automatic when there's a choice. Against a breath or a spell with a save, it comes once the save is rolled and before the damage lands, a passed save's half included; nothing is asked when no damage is dealt. Foes never cast it.
    • Counterspell is offered against any spell of level 1 or higher a foe casts, a stat block's Fireball included, to a hero or guest who sees the caster within 60 ft and can still react. The prompt shows the foe's save and its odds.
    • Counterspell chains. When a foe Counterspells your spell, any hero or guest who can see that foe within 60 ft and still has a Reaction and a way to cast Counterspell is asked whether to Counterspell it back. The prompt shows each side's Constitution save and its odds. Foes answer yours the same way, and the chain resolves from the last Counterspell back.
    • Eldritch Blast as a Reaction (War Caster, when a creature leaves your reach) sends one beam whatever your level: a Reaction is one attack, like the Opportunity Attack it replaces. The prompt and the log say so.

    Casting while exploring

    • Healing and other helpful spells can be cast outside a fight from the character sheet, on your heroes and on guests travelling with you. Slots, free castings, Concentration and lasting effects behave exactly as they do in a fight. That includes bringing back the dead: Revivify, Raise Dead, Reincarnate, Resurrection and True Resurrection, plus Gentle Repose to keep a body fresh (Reviving the Dead has every way back).
    • Spells from items are on the Spells tab too. Each worn or held item that's working for the hero (attuned, if it asks) has its own list, "From the" and the item's name, with its charges and what each spell costs: charges, a daily use, or at will. The ones that work outside a fight (a Staff of Healing's Cure Wounds, a Wand of Magic Detection's Detect Magic) get Cast or Cast on..., with a level pick where more charges buy a higher one. A spell that only works in a fight says so, and take the item off and its spells leave the list.
    • Spells that harm, push or summon wait for a fight, except the companions that stay with their caster: Find Familiar and Find Steed. Light, Dancing Lights, Continual Flame and Daylight are worth casting while exploring too, since their light lasts.
    • Many utility spells do something real while exploring:
      • Scouting: Augury, Divination, Commune, Clairvoyance and Arcane Eye tell you about the place you're in; Legend Lore and Scrying fill in the journal's bestiary; Find the Path lets you skip encounters on the road.
      • Getting through: Stone Shape and Passwall open doors, and Unseen Servant springs traps for you.
      • Carrying and brewing: Tenser's Floating Disk adds carrying capacity, Secret Chest opens your stash from anywhere, and Tasha's Bubbling Cauldron brews potions.
      • Helping a check: seven cantrips and ten spells give Advantage on the skills they suit, and Zone of Amicability makes 10 the lowest roll on influence checks.
      • Raising help: Animate Dead and Create Undead (at night only) can be cast while exploring, and the dead join the day's fights. Prayer of Healing, Regenerate and Foresight work outside fights too.
      • Warding a rest: Hallow, Forbiddance, Private Sanctum and Nondetection make a rest safe, and Tiny Hut and Guards and Wards let you Long Rest in a building that still has enemies in it (Resting and Saving).
    • Some conversations offer a spell as an option when a hero knows it. Speak with Animals is cast there as a Ritual (ten more minutes, no slot) and lets a beast tell you what it saw. Speak with Dead costs a slot of 3rd level or higher and works once on each corpse. Speak with Plants gets a garden or a row of vines to tell you what passed it in the last day (Talking Creatures).
    • Before a skill check outside a fight (a conversation's check, something in the world, a search, a lock, a climb, tending the dying, haggling), a hero proficient in the skill Helps (Advantage), and each hero who can cast Guidance is offered the chance to cast it on whoever's about to try. The d4 goes on that check. If a check in a place still fails, a hero with something to spend on it (Heroic Inspiration, a Bardic Inspiration die, a Fighter's Tactical Mind and the like) is asked before the failure lands; Let it stand takes the result. The big d20 throws a reroll as a new roll and names each bonus added.

    Components, focuses and scrolls

    • A material component with no listed cost, and a spellcasting focus, are assumed to be carried. A hand is always free for them and for gestures, whatever the caster is holding.
    • A component with a gold cost has to be in the inventory, and it's used up if the spell says so (a diamond for Revivify, for example). The churches in the village of Barovia, Vallaki and Krezk always sell diamonds and diamond dust (Reviving the Dead, Merchants and Services).
    • Spell scrolls are cast from the inventory (right-click, Cast) if the spell is on the reader's class list. A spell above the reader's own level needs a spellcasting check (DC 10 + the spell's level), and the scroll is used up either way.
    • Some magic items pass for something else until someone casts Identify on them from the item card.
