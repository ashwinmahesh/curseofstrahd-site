    Dinner at Castle Ravenloft

    Strahd sent his carriage with an invitation to dine. He kept his word: the table was laid, and he was waiting.

    Type
    Main quest
    Region
    Castle Ravenloft · level 10
    Given by
    Strahd von Zarovich
    Starts at
    Castle Ravenloft, the Main Floor

    Walkthrough

    Getting the quest

    The dinner only happens if you come to the castle as Strahd von Zarovich's invited guest. Three things have to be true first:

    • You have seen Strahd watching you from a ridge. That scene plays on your first journey after the burgomaster's burial (or after you have met Strahd).
    • The lowest-level hero in your party is level 9 or higher.
    • You arrive somewhere outdoors, anywhere but the castle itself or Death House.

    Then his driverless black carriage is waiting on the road, with a letter sealed in black wax on the seat. Choose Get in. The carriage takes you up the mountain (an hour and a half passes) and sets you down in the courtyard of Castle Ravenloft, the Gates, right in front of the keep's doors. If you say Not tonight. or Tear up the letter. (Thistle and Wren like that), the carriage comes back later, at least 48 hours apart, up to three times in all.

    If you walk up the castle road on your own instead (two hours from The River Ivlis Crossroads), you'll find the table laid for exactly as many of you as there are, and nobody at it. This quest never starts that way. When you came by invitation, Strahd waits at the organ in the dining hall at any hour until the meal is settled. The quest starts (Seated) the moment he turns round on the bench.

    Step by step

    1. From the courtyard, go through the keep's great doors into Castle Ravenloft, the Main Floor. Coming by carriage has one perk: the gargoyles on the gatehouse stay put for invited guests, even at night. If Ezmerelda travels with you, she stops by the keep's carved crest and gives you her four rules for the count's house (nothing rolls).
    2. Head west through the guests' hall to the dining hall. Strahd stops playing as you come in and greets you. What he says depends on what you have done: how you answered him at the burial, whether Ireena Kolyana is with you (or he already holds her), the fight with his wolves on the road, the beacon at Argynvostholt, whether anyone carries a Dark Gift from the Amber Temple, and what you learned about Marina. Godrick, Liriel, Thistle, Wren and Kip each get a line of their own. So do your allies: Ezmerelda d'Avenir if she travels with you, and Izek Strazni if he and Ireena both do. If Kasimir Velikov made his bargain at the Amber Temple, Strahd tells him his sister arrived ahead of him. Kasimir has no appetite: he leaves the party and sits on a stone by the road below the drawbridge, where you can ask him how long he has left and what to tell Patrina, or buy his scrolls. If Patrina was laid to rest or he made his peace, he keeps his chair.
    3. At the table you can ask as much as you like. None of these ends the meal:
      • Why did you invite us? and Tell us about this house. are just talk. He tells you to walk where you like tonight, and that the library is upstairs.
      • What do you want with Ireena? appears if she is with you or you have met her.
      • Medicine DC 13: Smell the wine. A success shows it is only very old wine. A failure tells you nothing, and the option stays, so you can try again.
      • Insight DC 17: Watch him. A success shows you what he wants from you: not to be bored. A failure is spent, and he teases you about the staring when he leaves.
      • (Cleric) Say grace before eating. No roll. Liriel +2, Ratatouille +1, and your cleric earns Heroic Inspiration.
      • With Ratatouille in the party, Taste the food, critically. Strahd offers him a title and a tower of his own. Have him refuse (Ratatouille +4, and Heroic Inspiration) rather than keep eating in silence (Ratatouille -1).
      • With Kip in the party and The Fine Print far enough along, Ask Strahd to sign something. Asking for just the signature moves The Fine Print to its signed stage (Kip +6). Letting Strahd buy Kip's contract instead makes Kip Strahd's debtor (Kip -6).
    4. End the meal with Thank him for his hospitality. or Draw on him. If you aren't ready to do either, choose (Excuse yourselves from the table for now.) He tells you to walk about and stretch your legs: the table will keep, and so will he. The meal isn't over and the quest stays at Seated. Talk to him at the organ again whenever you like; he greets you with "You came back. I had the soup kept warm." and you're back at the table.
    5. If you draw on him, Strahd and three Swarm of Bats fight you in the dining hall (13,150 XP, just past a High fight for four heroes at level 10). He fights until you bring him to 0 Hit Points, and then he leaves with a parting line instead of dying. He has 144, and until you break The Heart of Sorrow, the heart's ward soaks the first 50 damage he takes anywhere in the castle. Three times a day he answers a spell with Counterspell, and he spends it on the first spells he sees, so lead with cheap ones. The bats have 11 Hit Points each.

    While you are on the main floor:

    • The organ. Performance DC 16, "Play the count's organ" (each hero gets one try), reveals a compartment behind one of the stops holding an Instrument of the Bards (Anstruth Harp). You can also find it by searching (Investigation DC 16).
    • Containers. The sideboard (2 bottles of old wine, 25 gp), the servery cupboard (4 rations, 6 candles), the guest wardrobe (fine clothes, perfume) and the west turret's weapon rack (halberd, shield, 20 crossbow bolts).
    • The hearth that burns on nothing, at the far east end of the hall of faces. Put it out by unpicking its spell (Arcana DC 15, quiet; a failure closes this way), with a flask of Holy Water (quiet), or by shutting the flue (no roll, but two Guardian Portraits and three Helmed Horrors wake; two horrors below level 10). Then search beside the firebox (DC 13) for the seam. The hidden room behind it has an iron strongbox with 320 gp and an Animated Shield, plus a Tarokka treasure if your reading put one here.

    Choices and endings

    • Thank him (Dined, success). He thanks you for a charming evening, tells you the house is yours tonight, and vanishes from the bench. If you passed the Insight check he adds a warning: keep him interested, because nothing else has ever kept anyone safe in this house. Liriel +1, Ratatouille +1, Kip +1, Thistle -1.
    • Draw on him (Affront, failure). He fights until you beat him down, then goes. Thistle +2, Kip -2, Ratatouille -2. When he withdraws, the game remembers it: in the parley before your final battle he tells you that leaving a fight with you before was manners, not weakness.
    • Excuse yourselves (not an ending). Nothing is settled and nobody approves or disapproves. The table and the soup wait until you come back to thank him or draw on him.

    Whichever you choose, the rest of the castle is open to you. Once you've thanked him or drawn on him, Strahd isn't at the table again. The dinner's outcome otherwise only changes what the Narrator and your companions say about the dining hall.

    Getting around Castle Ravenloft

    The main floor is the hub. Every other part of the castle is one staircase away from it.

    • Castle Ravenloft, the Gates: the road, the drawbridge over the chasm, the portcullis and the courtyard. The keep's great doors lead north into the main floor. The side gate on the west opens onto the walk along the wall to Castle Ravenloft, the Overlook, a long balcony over the drop.
    • Castle Ravenloft, the Main Floor: you come in through the entry hall at the south end. North of it is the great entry, with the grand stairs up at its far end. West, through the guests' hall, is the dining hall, and behind it the servery with the servants' stairs down to the kitchens. East, a narrow door leads to the foot of the high tower stairway, and the gallery doors open into the hall of faces. A door on the south side of the hall of faces leads to the chapel passage, which has the doors of Castle Ravenloft, the Chapel and the old stair down to the crypts, bricked up for good (there's only a chink in the masonry beside it). A turret post stands on each side of the entry hall.
    • Up to the second floor, Castle Ravenloft, the Court of the Count: the grand stairs bring you onto the grand landing, where Rahadin greets you in the long King's Hall. Off it are the audience hall (north), the study (west), the trophy room (east), the accountant's office and the guardroom. Through the tower stair door at the east end are two staircases: the tower stairs up to the high tower's grand landing, and the stairs up to the third floor, Castle Ravenloft, the Rooms of Weeping (Strahd's bedroom and bath, the portrait gallery, the brides' lounge, the guest room and Rahadin's room). West of the hall of weeping is Castle Ravenloft, the West Wing: a passage of rat guards before a steel door, the count's wedding feast, a closet of capes, the Hall of Heroes, the maids' hall and the servants' attic. The Hall of Heroes and the window of Strahd's bedroom both open onto Castle Ravenloft, the Parapets. A secret stair behind a portrait of a young woman in the King's Hall goes down to Castle Ravenloft, the Treasury. It opens once you know about it (from Lief's ledger, Lief himself or Ludmilla) or find the catch (Investigation DC 16 or Perception DC 18).
    • Up the tower, Castle Ravenloft, the High Tower: a stair winding round an open well. It climbs from the tower's foot past the grand landing (Pidlwick II, and the arch to the Court of the Count) and the upper turns to the high landing. From the high landing, a stair goes up to Castle Ravenloft, the Heart of Sorrow, and a door leads into Castle Ravenloft, the Tower Rooms. From the tower rooms a ladder climbs to Castle Ravenloft, the Roofs, and a hidden stair behind Escher's wardrobe goes up to Castle Ravenloft, the Ravens' Roost. A hatch on the keep roof also drops into the roost. Once you've found the ladder behind the mirror in the west wing's servants' attic (Perception DC 15), it also opens from a panel on the tower stair: a second way between the tower and the third floor.
    • Down to Castle Ravenloft, the Larders of Ill Omen: the servants' stairs come down by the servants' hall and the kitchen. At the far east end, the jailers' stair has two ways on: the stairs down to Castle Ravenloft, the Dungeon, and the long stair down to the crypts, the only way down to them. The lift in the winch room also drops to the cells once you set its brake.
    • Down to Castle Ravenloft, the Catacombs: the long stair from the larders comes out at the east end of the upper gallery. The stair at the west end, up to the chapel side, is bricked up. In the middle, the hall of names holds the black stair down to Castle Ravenloft, Strahd's Tomb. At the south-west corner, stairs lead down to Castle Ravenloft, the Old Tombs (Sergei's tomb, the guardians' landing and the tomb of King Barov and Queen Ravenovia). A stair in the lower gallery's south wall goes down to Castle Ravenloft, the Lesser Vaults, nine more crypts along one passage; the humming niche in Saint Finderway's crypt at its east end opens into Strahd's tomb. On the lid in Saint Markovia's crypt, past a dart plate, lies her thighbone: Lift the thighbone from the lid. gives you Saint Markovia's Thighbone, a mace that crumbles after a fight in which it struck a vampire.

    The truce. From the moment you accept Strahd's invitation until dinner is over, the castle keeps a truce. The fights that start when you walk into a room wait, Strahd's final battles included, and resting in the castle is safe. Fights you start yourself (in a conversation, by opening something or by a check) still come. The truce ends at dinner, whether you thank him or affront him, and for good once you leave Castle Ravenloft: come back and every fight is as it was.

    The household. The castle's people walk its halls on their rounds: the Steward in the dining hall, Helga Ruvak in the Rooms of Weeping, the courtiers Lord Aubrey Thale, Lady Celestine Morrow, Boyar Vintila Crainic and Sir Lothar Brandt, ghostly servants such as Vlaicu the Lamplighter, and dead guards and kitchen hands. Each has a few words for you, and they step out of the way when a fight starts.

    Where the danger is. These fights start the moment you walk into the room. The numbers are for a party at level 10. Several are lighter below level 10, and most of the fights in the towers get heavier at level 11.

    Strahd himself only fights you outside his final battle in three places: at this dinner if you draw on him, at the top of the high tower when you break the Heart of Sorrow, and in his coffin if you try to stake him by day. His final battle waits in the one room Madam Eva's last card named (see Tarokka Reading and Where the Devil Waits): the chapel, the overlook, the audience hall, the study, the treasury, the Heart of Sorrow, the hall of bones, the black horse's crypt in the catacombs, Sergei's tomb, the royal tomb or his own tomb. Walking into that room after your reading starts it, once he has named his price.

    Resting. A rest is risky in most of the castle, and impossible in the Rooms of Weeping, the parapets, the treasury, the dungeon, the Heart of Sorrow, the roofs, the overlook and Strahd's tomb.

    If you're stuck

    • Nobody at the table. You came without the carriage. Strahd only hosts guests who accepted his invitation, and the invitation never comes inside the castle. Leave, travel somewhere outdoors, and wait for the carriage.
    • The carriage never comes. Check that the lowest-level hero is level 9, and that you have seen Strahd on the ridge. After three refusals it stops coming, and this quest can't be started any more.

    Journal

    ❝Every journal entry and hint

    Seated: Strahd received us in his dining hall, at the organ, as if we were old friends late for supper. The food is real. The host is something else.
    Hint: Sit at Strahd's table on the castle's main floor and see the meal through, politely or not.

    Dined (the end: success): We ate at Strahd's table and he let us go, which he did not have to do. He left us the run of his house for the night and went off into it himself.

    Affront (the end: failure): We drew steel at Strahd's table. He left us his bats and went, laughing, as if we had paid him a compliment.
